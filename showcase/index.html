spotify-poller

A custody report over a self-hosted SQLite copy of a personal Spotify listening history. Next.js, Tailwind, JWT sessions, Docker — proves collection is running, surfaces gaps instead of hiding them, and lets the operator browse and export what was collected.

Audience measurement

Visits are counted with Umami, self-hosted by the publisher at insight.steevepommier.com: the data is gathered there and nowhere else, no third party receives it, and none of it is used for advertising.

What is collected, for each view of this page: the page viewed (its path alone, without query string or fragment), the referrer (the site you came from), your browser, your operating system, your device type and screen size, your browser’s language, and your country, derived from your IP address. No event is sent: nothing you do on the page is reported.

What never is: no cookie is set, no personal data is collected, your IP address is not stored, and nothing can follow you from one site to another.

How to decline: if your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, or if you switch counting off below, the counter is not even loaded and nothing is sent. The switch keeps your choice in this browser, under the key umami.disabled. Visits are only counted on costardrouge.github.io.

Without JavaScript, this page cannot tell whether your visits are counted; the counter does not run without it either.

What else this page knows about you

Nothing. It loads no other script and no other third-party resource, and stores nothing in your browser but the switch above. The screenshots show the application over fixture data generated in its build pipeline — no real Spotify account and no real listening history were involved.
